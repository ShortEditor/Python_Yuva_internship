This Week 2 report presents a comprehensive Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and visualization study of the Titanic passenger dataset using Python. The analysis continues from the cleaned dataset prepared during Week 1, allowing the project to focus on discovering meaningful patterns and relationships rather than repeating the data-cleaning process. Pandas was used for data loading, descriptive statistics, grouping, aggregation, feature transformation, and correlation analysis, while Matplotlib was used to create clear, annotated visualizations.

The EDA begins with an inspection of the dataset structure and descriptive statistics for important numerical variables such as Age, Fare, FamilySize, SibSp, and Parch. The report then investigates survival patterns across passenger sex, passenger class, age group, embarkation port, and travel-alone status. Multiple visualizations are included to communicate these patterns, including bar charts, distribution histograms, a sex-by-class heatmap, a family-size trend, and a numerical correlation matrix.

Several transformations and aggregations were performed to make the analysis more interpretable. FamilySize and IsAlone were reused as meaningful engineered variables from Week 1, while TicketGroupSize and FarePerPerson were derived specifically for EDA. The report also explains why the highly right-skewed Fare variable should be interpreted carefully and why median-based summaries can be more representative than the mean.

The analysis found strong descriptive differences in survival by sex and passenger class, with female and higher-class passengers showing substantially higher observed survival rates. Traveling with family was also associated with a higher survival rate than traveling alone. At the same time, the report critically discusses limitations, including subgroup sizes, confounding relationships, historical context, and the distinction between association and causation. A reproducible Python script and all report figures are included with the Word document.
